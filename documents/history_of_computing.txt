The History of Computing

Charles Babbage designed the Analytical Engine in the nineteenth century.
Ada Lovelace wrote what is considered the first algorithm intended for a machine.

Alan Turing formalized computation with the Turing machine in 1936.
The Turing machine is a mathematical model that defines what is computable.

The ENIAC, completed in 1945, was among the first electronic general-purpose
computers. It filled a room and consumed enormous power.

Transistors replaced vacuum tubes, and integrated circuits packed thousands
of components onto a single chip. Moore observed that transistor density
doubled roughly every two years.

The personal computer arrived in the late seventies. Graphical interfaces,
the mouse, and the spreadsheet transformed computing into a mass product.

The World Wide Web, proposed by Tim Berners-Lee in 1989, connected documents
across the globe with hyperlinks. Search engines such as AltaVista and later
Google made the web navigable, and the inverted index became the most
important data structure of the information age.
